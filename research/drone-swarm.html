Drone Swarm
via Motor Imagery
One mind, many machines: flying a swarm of small drones with the same imagined-movement signals that drive our wheelchair. A planned project.
Overview

Our wheelchair project answers a focused question: can one person steer one machine with imagined movement alone? This project asks the natural next one: can one person direct many? A drone swarm is the ideal testbed. Flight is unforgiving of latency and misclassification, so it pushes our decoding to be faster and more reliable — and a swarm forces us to think beyond "brain as joystick" toward "brain as conductor," where the user expresses high-level intent and the machines coordinate the details among themselves.

There is also a more human motivation. For someone who has never left a bed or a chair under their own power, telepresence through a swarm of flying machines is a genuinely new kind of embodiment — a way to move through space, look around, and explore that no wheelchair can offer.

In plainer terms: we want to close the loop between a human brain and a flock of aircraft. The pilot wears a non-invasive EEG headset and imagines movement — the same motor imagery signals we already decode for the wheelchair — and the swarm responds: advancing, turning, holding formation. The video above captures the feeling we are chasing.

The approach

We are not starting from zero. The signal-processing pipeline, classifiers, and training tools built for the EEG wheelchair are designed to be reusable; a drone swarm is, from the decoder's point of view, just a different output device. That reuse is deliberate — every project in the club should make the next one cheaper to build.

The plan is to grow in stages. First, a single small drone flown with discrete imagined commands, mirroring the wheelchair's forward/rotate/rest scheme. Then a handful of drones holding formation autonomously while the pilot steers the formation as a single unit. The swarm handles low-level stability and collision avoidance on its own — the human supplies intent, not stick inputs.

Status

This project is in the planning stage. We are currently scoping hardware platforms, indoor flight-testing space, and how much of the wheelchair decoder can be carried over unchanged. If you are interested in supporting or collaborating on this work, we would like to hear from you.
