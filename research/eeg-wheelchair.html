An electric wheelchair add-on driven by three motor imagery classes: imagined foot movement (forward), right hand (right turn), and rest. Built entirely by undergraduates on commodity hardware.
// overview

An electric wheelchair add-on that translates motor imagery signals into joystick control commands, enabling hands-free navigation. The device is designed to mount on top of any standard electric wheelchair joystick.

--- paragraph 2 ver 1

The initial prototype uses three classes: feet (forward), right hand (rotate right), and rest. This is a deliberate choice to minimize system complexity and prioritize a functional, usable prototype over premature feature expansion. The final product will support user-configurable handedness (right or left rotation) or full 4-class control: feet (forward), right hand (rotate right), left hand (rotate left), and rest.

--- paragraph 2 ver 2

We work with feet/right hand/rest while prototyping, the final product will allow the user to either choose between rotating right with right hand or rotating left with left hand, or just using a 4-way classification, go forward (feet), rotate right (right hand), rotate left (left hand), rest.

The design choice of strarting with "go forward, rotate right and rest" is intentional, and meant to minimize the complexity of the system to focus on creating a working and easy to use prototype instead of getting distracted by flattering unnecessary features.

// signal processing pipeline  → full software page

We begin with conventional classification models such as LDA and SVM with CSP features, as they are lightweight and predictable. If these hit a wall in accuracy, we will move to deep learning approaches like CNNs or transformers.

All models are benchmarked against MOABB (Mother of All BCI Benchmarks) for standardized comparison.
